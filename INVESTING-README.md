# How Conviction Picks Stocks — In Plain Words

Conviction looks for one specific situation: **a good business whose stock price has just started moving up.**

Think of it like hiring. A resume tells you if someone is capable (the business). An interview tells you if they're ready right now (the price action). You want both. Conviction rejects stocks that pass only one test.

---

## The two questions it asks about every stock

### 1. Is this a good business? (the "fundamental" side — 60% of the score)

It reads the company's actual financial reports and checks:

| What it checks | Why it matters | The simple idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Sales growth** (last 3 years) | A business that sells more every year is winning customers | Is the shop getting more crowded? |
| **Profit growth** (earnings per share) | Growing sales are pointless if profits don't follow | Is the owner taking home more? |
| **Profit margin, and is it improving** | High margin = pricing power; rising margin = getting stronger | Out of every ₹100 of sales, how much is kept — and is that number growing? |
| **Return on equity (ROE)** | How well the company uses shareholders' money | If you gave them ₹100, how much profit do they make on it? |
| **Debt vs equity** | Heavy debt can sink even a growing company in a bad year | Is the house bought with savings or a huge loan? |
| **Free cash flow** | Profits on paper can be fake; cash in the bank cannot | After all bills, does real money pile up? |
| **Valuation (P/E, PEG)** | A great company at a crazy price is still a bad deal | Are you paying ₹80 for something worth ₹40? |
| **Moat / leadership** (rated by hand) | Leaders with an advantage stay profitable longer | Can a competitor easily copy this business? |

Each item earns points; together they make a **Fundamental score out of 100**.

### 2. Is the price ready right now? (the "technical" side — 40% of the score)

Even great companies can see their stock go nowhere for years. So Conviction also studies the price chart, looking for one of two "wake-up" patterns:

**Pattern A — Turnaround (reversal).** The stock was falling, but the fall has clearly stopped and buyers are back:
- Price climbs back above its 50-day average (the crowd's recent "fair price")
- Each dip stops at a higher level than the last dip
- Momentum meters (RSI, MACD) flip from weak to strong
- Buying-pressure candle patterns appear

**Pattern B — Breakout (continuation).** The stock rested quietly after a rise, and has now burst out of that rest:
- Price closes above the ceiling it was stuck under for weeks
- The resting phase got tighter and calmer (sellers ran out)
- The breakout day has unusually heavy trading volume (big money confirming)
- Price is near its 52-week high — strength, not weakness

Whichever pattern is clearer becomes the stock's **Technical score out of 100**. No pattern = the stock is dropped, however good the company is. Why? Because "cheap and ignored" can stay cheap and ignored for a very long time; we want quality that the market has *started* to notice.

---

## Putting it together

**Final score = 40% technical + 60% fundamental** (you can move this slider).

Fundamentals get the bigger weight because this is a *buy-and-hold* tool — over years, business quality decides your return; the chart only decides your starting point.

Before scoring, every stock must also pass hard gates based on your risk setting: minimum company size, maximum debt, minimum ROE, and a minimum final score. Conservative mode keeps only large, low-debt, high-quality names; aggressive mode lets smaller and rougher names through.

The survivors are ranked, and the top ones are shown with:
- **why** they qualified (exact signals and numbers),
- a **buy zone** (roughly: between the 20-day average and just above today's price — so you're not chasing a spike),
- an **exit-if-wrong level** (if price falls this far, the turnaround/breakout has failed — reconsider),
- a rough **3-year value idea** based on profit growth continuing, and
- the **risks** specific to that stock.

## Where the data comes from, honestly

- **Prices:** official NSE end-of-day files, updated every evening automatically.
- **Financials:** Yahoo Finance company reports, refreshed automatically. Usually right, occasionally stale for smaller companies — cards flag when something is missing so you can verify on Screener.in.
- **Universe:** the Nifty 500 (India's 500 largest listed companies).
- Nothing is live/intraday, and nothing is predicted — every number shown actually happened.

---

## What this tool is NOT

- It is **not advice**. It's a filter that shortens a list of 500 to a handful worth *your own research*.
- It **cannot see the future**. Breakouts fail. Turnarounds relapse. Good companies stumble. In a market-wide crash, nearly everything falls regardless of scores.
- It **cannot judge people or news** — fraud, management quality, lawsuits, and industry shifts are invisible to it. That's your homework: read the annual report, check promoter holding, search recent news.
- Scores are mechanical. A 78 is not "better guaranteed" than a 71.

**Golden rules:** never buy just because it appears here; spread your money across several ideas; size positions so one failure doesn't hurt; and if you're unsure, talk to a SEBI-registered investment adviser.
